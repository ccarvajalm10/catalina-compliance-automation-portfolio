## How this eval works

1. **Gold set** — [`gold.jsonl`](./gold.jsonl), 30 Annex A controls hand-labelled from the
   sample company's documents by the project author, each with a one-line justification.
   It is the assessment an ISO 27001 lead implementer would defend in an audit.
2. **Live run** — `scripts/run-live.ts` runs the real pipeline (`lib/analyzer.ts`) over the
   sample company and writes `.eval/live-result.json`.
3. **Scoring** — `evals/score.mjs` compares the live verdicts to the gold labels and reports:
   - **Exact verdict accuracy** — the strict metric.
   - **Severity-collapsed accuracy** — Met/Partial/Gap mapped to 2/1/0; a Partial-vs-Met
     disagreement counts as a half-miss, a Met-vs-Gap as a full miss. This reflects that in
     practice a Partial-vs-Met slip costs a reviewer far less than a missed Gap.
   - **Per-verdict precision / recall / F1** and a **confusion matrix**.
   - A **miss table** listing every disagreement with the gold rationale, so failures can be
     inspected rather than hidden behind an average.

## Why measure this at all

A gap analysis that is 80% right but doesn't tell you *which* 20% is wrong is not useful for
compliance work. The value proposition of the tool is **triage**: it does the first pass so a
human reviews 93 pre-drafted verdicts with citations instead of starting from a blank sheet.
The eval exists to state honestly how good that first pass is, and where it is weakest
(historically: Partial-vs-Met boundary calls, and controls that depend on evidence of
*operation* rather than *existence*).
